# Opportunity Discovery

Find problems worth investigating and develop useful starting points for ideas.

## Process

1. **Read the context.** Inspect the field map, relevant papers, project constraints, and prior decisions.
2. **Look for opportunities.** Consider practical bottlenecks, desired capabilities, expensive or fragile methods, useful component combinations, measurement gaps, conflicting findings, and unexplained observations.
3. **Ground the problem.** Connect factual statements to papers, measurements, or concrete task requirements. Separate observed limitations from proposed opportunities. Check differing experimental conditions before treating findings as contradictory.
4. **Develop candidates.** Describe what could change, why it could help, and what useful result would follow. Give promising ideas enough detail to assess their potential.
5. **Identify known neighbors.** Record relevant prior work already available. Use `novelty-audit` for focused closest-work searches after candidates are developed.
6. **Check initial feasibility.** Sketch what could be measured and which resources are needed for a first test. Prioritize a small set of concrete opportunities.

## Output

For each candidate, include the problem, importance, proposed approach or open question, rationale, relevant sources or task requirements, closest known work, and an initial test. Record missing information that affects the next action.

Simple changes and combinations can support valuable research. Judge them by the problem they address, the difference from prior work, and the value of a successful result. A proposed opportunity may be worth exploring before it has supporting experimental results.

## Completion criterion

Each candidate has a concrete motivation, an explanation of what is worth trying, and a practical route to learning whether it helps. The developed candidates are ready for focused comparison with prior work.
