# Novelty Taxonomy

Novelty is multidimensional. Compare the proposed contribution against the closest prior work on each relevant dimension.

## Dimensions

- **Problem novelty:** a materially different question, setting, or phenomenon is investigated.
- **Mechanism novelty:** a new explanatory mechanism or causal account is proposed and tested.
- **Method novelty:** a new procedure, architecture, algorithm, or design is introduced.
- **Theoretical novelty:** a new theorem, formalization, bound, or conceptual framework is established.
- **Empirical novelty:** a previously unknown and robust finding is demonstrated.
- **Evaluation novelty:** a new measurement, benchmark, protocol, or evaluation lens reveals something existing evaluations miss.
- **Boundary novelty:** a meaningful operating boundary, failure mode, or phase transition is identified.
- **Synthesis novelty:** previously separate findings are connected in a way that yields a new explanation or prediction.

A contribution can be novel in one dimension and incremental in another. State exactly which dimension carries the contribution.

## Combinations and simple changes

Judge a combination or small modification by its contribution. Useful outcomes include:
- improved performance, efficiency, reliability, or usability under a meaningful comparison;
- creates an emergent capability not present in the components separately;
- reveals a new interaction or mechanism;
- enables a decisive test of a previously unresolved question;
- changes a meaningful theoretical or empirical conclusion;
- establishes a new, generalizable boundary.

State which problem the combination addresses, why it could help, and what would count as a useful result. Develop the candidate before evaluating it against closest work; theoretical novelty is one possible contribution type.

## Comparison protocol

For each closest prior work, compare:
1. research question and setting;
2. core claim;
3. mechanism or method;
4. assumptions;
5. evidence and evaluation;
6. demonstrated boundary;
7. what the candidate adds that the prior work does not.

Use concrete differences, not adjectives such as "more general", "more efficient", or "more robust" without a defined condition and evidence.

## Audit outcomes

- `substantiated`: available primary-source evidence supports a clear, material difference.
- `plausible_unverified`: a meaningful difference is plausible, but the search or evidence is incomplete.
- `incremental`: a difference exists but appears to be a modest extension within an established line.
- `anticipated`: prior work already contains the central idea or its essential mechanism.
- `contradicted`: evidence undermines the proposed novelty claim.
- `not_assessable`: the idea or corpus is too vague or incomplete to judge.

Record the search scope with the judgment. An incremental novelty status can still support pursuing a valuable empirical improvement.
