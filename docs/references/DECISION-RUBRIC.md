# Research Direction Decision Rubric

Compare candidates primarily by the problem worth solving and the quality of the proposed solution. Give a short rationale for each relevant dimension rather than an aggregate score.

## Readiness to recommend a direction

A developed candidate has:
- a concrete, worthwhile problem grounded in task needs, observations, or literature;
- a method whose key steps address the identified difficulty;
- a reasonable design rationale and a worked example;
- a substantive account of its difference from closest work;
- a useful expected contribution and basic resource feasibility.

An unresolved central design choice calls for further method development. Detailed experiment planning is a follow-on stage, not a condition for recommending a direction.

## Comparison dimensions

1. **Problem value:** What limitation or questioned assumption matters, to whom, and with what consequence?
2. **Method fit and simplicity:** How cleanly do the proposed operations address that difficulty? Does every component earn its keep, or are parts added only to avoid overlap with prior papers? Does the worked example expose a missing step?
3. **Difference from prior work:** What is already solved, what does the proposal add or simplify, and why does that difference matter?
4. **Rationale:** Why should the design help? Assess formal or causal depth when that carries the contribution.
5. **Basic feasibility:** Are the essential data, model access, and resources available? Which constraint changes the choice?

When choosing an experiment, additionally assess whether it informs the next decision and whether it can be performed with the available resources. Ease of experimentation alone does not establish a direction's research value.

A method's simplicity is a strength when it solves a real problem or removes an unnecessary assumption. Evaluate simplifications, combinations, training strategies, losses, and prompts using the same dimensions as other proposals.

## Recommendation labels

- `pursue`: the problem, method, and potential contribution justify further work.
- `refine`: the problem, method, or difference from known work needs sharpening.
- `defer`: a specific prerequisite or resource constraint blocks useful progress.
- `reject`: prior work supplies the intended contribution, evidence undermines the rationale, or the proposed result would not be useful.

Include the reason, the next useful action, and any information that would change the recommendation. The next action may be problem clarification, method development, a targeted literature check, or an experiment appropriate to the current stage.
